# Save With Property: Telegram tip bank

Source: Joel's 2nd Brain frameworks. Tip posts for the channel "Save With Property | Joel Li" are drawn ONLY from this list, one tip per post, in order of least recently used (check the queue sheet ids, which carry the tip code, e.g. 2026-11-03_2_tip_T01).

## How to write a tip post (the objective: members want to come to Joel directly)
- Give the WHAT and the WHY clearly, so the member learns something real and trusts the thinking.
- Hold back the HOW for their own case: the exact numbers, which project or stack, whether it fits their situation. That judgment is what Joel does 1:1. Make the gap visible and natural, never a tease or a withheld secret.
- Structure: bold hook question naming the member's situation; the common mistake most people make; the principle in 2 or 3 short points (with one anonymised example or a simple number if it helps); then the bridge: "Whether this works for you depends on ..." naming the 1 or 2 personal factors; then a gentle CTA framed as how Joel can help (e.g. "Send me your block and flat type and I will show you where you stand").
- Under 160 words. Joel's voice: short declarative sentences, calm, authority led, probability language, no hype words (opportunity, unique, act fast, limited time, trust me), no hyphens or dashes, close on a question or a clear next step.
- Never name clients. Never give a client's exact deal figures; round and anonymise ("a buyer on the 10th floor", "about $800K"). Never promise returns. CEA safe.
- Any rule or rate quoted (ABSD, BSD, LTV, TDSR, MSR, CPF usage, SORA) must be current and checked on two official or reputable sources that day.

## Buying
- T01 Know your exit before you pay the entry (F29). The price you sell at later is decided by the price you buy at today. Check that someone has already paid your future exit price for a comparable unit. Bridge: which units in your shortlist pass this test.
- T02 Buy where sellers are crowded (F01, F31). When many owners of a young project or EC list at the same time (TOP, or EC MOP after 5 years), buyers hold the leverage. The window closes in 12 to 24 months. Bridge: which projects are in that window now.
- T03 Big units at launch, small units at resale (F32). At launch big units are often priced at a lower psf; in resale, demand can flip. Bridge: whether the project you are eyeing shows this gap.
- T04 The low floor gap (F32, F09). At launch the floor premium is large; at resale the difference is usually small. Price jumps are not even per floor; buy just below the jump. Bridge: where the jump sits in a specific price list.
- T05 Same price, more central (F23). Before buying in the OCR, check what the same budget buys in the RCR. Bridge: the side by side for their budget.
- T06 The 90 second corridor check (F34). Neighbour's door open with smoke smell, shoes and items spilling out, ball marks on walls: signals of what living there will feel like. Bridge: what else to look for during a viewing.
- T07 A vs B projects (F06). If everyone bought in at similar prices, no one can easily undercut you later. Scattered entry prices mean undercut risk. Bridge: whether a given project is A or B.
- T08 Developer stress is buyer leverage (F03, F08). Slow sales and many units unsold give buyers room; early tranches are often priced to move. Bridge: reading a project's sell through.

## Selling
- T09 Don't sell into a crowd (F31). If a wave of TOP or MOP listings is about to hit your project, time your sale before it or after it, not during it. Bridge: where your project is in that cycle.
- T10 Sell high, buy low (F07). When resale prices are firm and new launch pricing is reasonable, the window to sell your resale and buy new does not stay open. Bridge: the numbers for their current home and next move.
- T11 The 10 second feel (F20). Buyers decide how a home feels within seconds of walking in. Presentation, light, smell and clear space are what lift the offer. Bridge: what to fix first in their unit.
- T12 Price on proof, not on records (F05, Signal 22). A headline record tells you what the very top paid, not what your unit fetches. Price off the transactions that truly compare. Bridge: their real comparables.

## Financing
- T13 Waiting for rates can cost more (F10, F25). Saving on interest can be wiped out many times over if prices move first. Use the current SORA environment as context. Bridge: the trade off on their loan size.
- T14 The show fund (F35). Fixed deposits or shares can be pledged to close a loan shortfall; after the lock in, refinance and the funds are released. Bridge: whether their profile qualifies and how much is needed.
- T15 A second name on the loan (F35). A co-borrower can raise the loan quantum and bring in their CPF OA (needs about 3 months of payslips). Bridge: what that does to their budget.
- T16 Fixed vs floating and when to reprice (F10). Know your lock in end date, your spread, and what a 0.5% move costs you monthly. Bridge: running those three numbers.
- T17 The tenant pays your principal (F30). Rent minus interest minus MCST: part builds equity, part is real cash flow. Keep them separate. Bridge: the honest split for their unit.

## Owning and upgrading
- T18 Upgrading doesn't always mean spending more (F33). Same price, but bigger, more central, freehold, or younger lease. Old to young is the most underused. Bridge: which upgrade type fits them.
- T19 Decoupling (F35). Joint owners can restructure so one can buy a second property as a first timer, without ABSD; BSD on the share transfer applies. Bridge: whether the sums work for their household.
- T20 Watch your lease against the CPF threshold (Assessment B). As the lease shortens, fewer buyers can use full CPF, which narrows your future buyer pool. Bridge: where their lease stands.
- T21 Macro before micro (F02, F04, F25). Read what government signals (GLS supply, policy changes, URA plans) are saying before choosing a project. Bridge: what today's signals mean for their next move.
