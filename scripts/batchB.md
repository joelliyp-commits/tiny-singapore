## v048 Buying a new launch: when you actually pay (skill, CTA follow) - Tue 4 Nov 6pm
1. You buy a 1.5 million dollar new launch. You don't pay it all at once.
2. 5% when you get the option. Another 15% within 8 weeks. That's 300,000 dollars, mostly cash and CPF.
3. Then the bank pays as the building goes up: 10% at foundation, 10% at the frame.
4. 5% each for walls, roof, doors and windows, then car parks and roads.
5. 25% at TOP, when you get your keys. The last 15% at completion.
6. So your loan, and your monthly instalment, grows over the years, not on day one.
7. Useful info? Follow for more.
Disclaimer: Normal progressive payment scheme for uncompleted private homes.

## v050 Sold your flat, got less cash than expected? (skill, CTA follow) - Wed 5 Nov 6pm
1. You sell your flat for 600,000 dollars. You expected a big cheque. It's much smaller.
2. Every CPF dollar you used for your home must go back to your CPF account first.
3. Plus the interest it would have earned: 2.5% a year, compounded.
4. Used 200,000 dollars over 15 years? You could owe CPF close to 290,000 dollars.
5. It's not lost. It goes back into your own CPF. But it's not cash in hand.
6. Planning your next home? Count your cash proceeds after the CPF refund, not before.
7. Useful info? Follow for more.
Disclaimer: Illustration assumes a lump sum used at the start. Check your actual figure on the CPF website.

2. It's called the resale levy. It's how HDB takes back part of the first subsidy.
3. Sold a 3 room? 30,000 dollars. A 4 room? 40,000. A 5 room? 45,000.
4. An executive flat, 50,000. An EC, 55,000.
5. It can't be paid with CPF. It comes from cash or your sale proceeds.
6. Planning a second BTO or EC? Count the levy in your budget from day one.

## v053 Your maintenance fee isn't the same as your neighbour's (skill, CTA save) - Sat 8 Nov 6pm
1. You're buying a condo. Your maintenance fee is higher than your neighbour's. Why?
2. Fees are shared by share value, which depends on the size of your unit.
3. Bigger units pay more. Penthouses pay the most.
4. Part goes to running costs. Part goes to the sinking fund, for big repairs later.
5. Fund too low when the lifts need replacing? Owners can be asked for a special levy, also by share value.
6. Before you buy, ask for the latest financial statements and the sinking fund balance.
7. Save this before your next viewing.

6. So in older estates, fewer neighbours can block a sale.
